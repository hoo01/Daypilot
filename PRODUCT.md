# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Individuals who want a single, intelligent place to manage the full texture of their day: work tasks, health routines, energy levels, mood, and personal commitments. They're not power-users of productivity apps — they want something that thinks with them, not something they have to configure. Primary use is daily: morning check-in, midday replan, end-of-day review.

## Product Purpose

DayPilot is an AI-powered daily life OS. It builds a personalised schedule each day, adapts it as circumstances change, and uses chat as the primary interface for replanning. Success means users feel genuinely supported — less decision fatigue, more follow-through, a day that reflects their real priorities and energy.

## Brand Personality

Warm · Supportive · Personal. DayPilot feels like a trusted companion who knows your rhythms. It speaks plainly, adapts to how you're feeling, and never lectures. The interface should feel focused and calm — a space where you can think clearly, not a dashboard that demands attention.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboard: no navy + white + purple gradient buttons, no hero-metric card grids, no Notion/Linear clone aesthetics, no "Product of the Day" visual language.
- Bubbly wellness apps: not pastel, not rounded-to-the-point-of-cartoonish, not Instagram-adjacent.
- Cold dev tooling: the terminal aesthetic is acceptable only if it retains warmth — not bleak grey-on-grey, not alienating for non-technical users.

## Design Principles

1. **Presence without pressure.** The AI feels like it's always there, ready — but never demanding. No notification anxiety, no urgency theatre.
2. **Clarity over completeness.** Show only what matters for this moment in the day. Hide what the user doesn't need right now.
3. **Intimate, not clinical.** Personal data (mood, health, routines) should feel like a quiet conversation, not a medical dashboard.
4. **Focus earns trust.** Every visual choice should reduce noise. The interface is calm because the user's day is not.
5. **Warmth survives constraints.** Even a minimal, dark, type-forward design can feel warm. That warmth lives in copy tone, color temperature, and generous spacing — not decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Text contrast ≥4.5:1 for body, ≥3:1 for large text. Keyboard-navigable primary flows. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No information conveyed by color alone.
